\begin{abstract}
% TODO WS5 (write last, ~150-200 words): objectives, approach, key findings.
Small retailers and independent convenience stores often face significant barriers when attempting to digitize inventory for online sale. Creating a product listing requires multiple manual tasks including product identification, pricing lookup, image acquisition, category assignment, and description writing. This project presents Snap-to-Sell, an open-source artificial intelligence pipeline capable of transforming a single smartphone photograph of a packaged retail product into a draft e-commerce listing. The system combines vision-language reasoning, optical character recognition (OCR), retrieval-grounded product search, automated listing generation, image enhancement, and responsible artificial intelligence guardrails. Evaluation was conducted on 58 packaged consumer products spanning food, beverage, household, personal-care, tobacco, and alcohol categories. Results demonstrate improvements over a baseline listing-generation workflow in product identification, category classification, restricted-item detection, and image enhancement. Snap-to-Sell achieved a top-1 product-recognition accuracy of 86.2\%, category accuracy of 79.3\%, age-restricted item recall of 100\%, and upgraded 29.3\% of product images. Price estimation emerged as the system's primary limitation due to retrieval ambiguity and multipack matching errors. The findings suggest that retrieval-grounded multimodal systems can substantially reduce listing-creation effort while maintaining human oversight and transparency.

\end{abstract}

\subsection{Background, Context, and Scope}

The rapid growth of e-commerce has transformed how products are discovered, marketed, and sold. While large retailers maintain sophisticated product-information systems and dedicated content-management teams, independent retailers and corner stores often rely on manual workflows to create product listings. These workflows involve product identification, image collection, pricing research, inventory categorization, and listing creation.

Recent developments in multimodal artificial intelligence have created opportunities to automate many of these tasks. Vision-language models (VLMs) can recognize objects within images, optical character recognition systems can extract packaging text, and retrieval-augmented systems can ground generated outputs using external sources of factual information. Together, these technologies create the possibility of generating e-commerce listings directly from product photographs.

This project focuses on packaged retail products typically found in convenience stores and small independent retailers. The goal is to evaluate whether a single smartphone photograph can be transformed into a useful, publication-ready e-commerce listing using open-source technologies and publicly available data sources.

\subsection{Problem Statement and Motivation}

The digitization of physical inventory remains challenging for small retailers because creating product listings is both time-consuming and repetitive. Even a simple item requires a retailer to identify the product, locate suitable product information, determine an appropriate price, select a category, and provide high-quality product imagery.

Errors in product identification can propagate throughout the workflow, producing incorrect pricing, inaccurate descriptions, and misleading product images. Because many products share visually similar packaging, product recognition remains difficult.

The research problem explored in this study is whether a single smartphone photograph can be transformed into an accurate and useful e-commerce listing while maintaining transparency and human oversight.

\subsection{Research Question and Objectives}

The central research question investigated in this project is:

\textit{Can a single smartphone photograph of a packaged retail product be converted into a useful e-commerce listing through the use of vision-language models, retrieval grounding, and responsible AI safeguards?}

The project objectives are:

\begin{itemize}
\item Develop an end-to-end retail listing generation pipeline.
\item Evaluate multimodal product identification.
\item Compare retrieval-grounded listing generation against a baseline workflow.
\item Assess image-enhancement capabilities.
\item Investigate pricing-performance limitations.
\item Implement confidence-aware responsible-AI safeguards.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Project Contributions}

This project makes five contributions.

First, it proposes a fully open-source retail digitization pipeline aimed at small retailers.

Second, it combines vision-language reasoning and OCR for product identification.

Third, it introduces retrieval-grounded metadata generation through open product databases and shopping-search systems.

Fourth, it implements catalogue-image replacement validated through similarity matching.

Finally, it introduces responsible-AI guardrails that route uncertain predictions to human review.

\subsection{Product Recognition and Retail Classification}

Retail-product recognition has attracted significant research attention due to the growing importance of inventory management and e-commerce automation. Datasets such as RP2K and CAPG-GP demonstrate the challenges associated with visually similar consumer products, packaging variations, and changing retail inventories. Fine-grained recognition therefore remains more difficult than traditional object classification.

\subsection{Vision-Language Models for Product Understanding}

Vision-language models such as CLIP have demonstrated strong zero-shot recognition capabilities. By learning aligned image and text representations, these models can identify products through semantic similarity rather than traditional category classification alone. These capabilities make VLMs attractive for product identification tasks where labeled data may be limited.

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Product Search}

Retrieval-grounded systems have emerged as a method for reducing hallucinations and improving factual consistency. Rather than relying solely on model parameters, retrieval pipelines first search external databases and then generate outputs grounded in retrieved evidence. This approach is particularly relevant to e-commerce applications where factual accuracy is required.

\subsection{Price Estimation and Product Matching}

Price-estimation research has explored both image-based regression and retrieval-based approaches. While retrieval grounding typically improves explainability, it introduces dependency on the quality of external search results, making product disambiguation a critical challenge.

\subsection{Responsible AI in Retail Applications}

Responsible AI research emphasizes safety, transparency, fairness, and human oversight. Retail systems present unique risks because incorrect information may influence purchases and create customer trust issues. Consequently, modern retail-AI systems increasingly incorporate confidence estimation, validation checks, and human-review mechanisms.

\subsection{End-to-End Workflow}

Snap-to-Sell follows a six-stage workflow consisting of product capture, image preprocessing, product recognition, retrieval grounding, listing generation, and confidence validation.

The workflow begins when a user photographs a retail product using a smartphone. The image is then preprocessed before being passed through multimodal recognition components. Product metadata is subsequently retrieved from external sources and incorporated into a generated product listing. Finally, guardrails assess confidence and route uncertain outputs to human review.

\subsection{Dataset Collection}

The evaluation dataset consisted of 58 packaged consumer products collected from retail environments. The test set included products from food, beverage, household, personal-care, tobacco, and alcohol categories.

\subsection{Image Preprocessing}

Images underwent normalization, resizing, and quality improvements using OpenCV-based techniques. These transformations improved OCR readability and recognition consistency across varying lighting conditions.

\subsection{Vision-Language Product Recognition}

Product recognition utilized multimodal reasoning combining image content and packaging text. This approach reduced ambiguity by supplementing visual features with textual information extracted directly from product packaging.

\subsection{OCR-Based Information Extraction}

OCR was applied to extract product names, brand identifiers, and descriptive packaging text. These extracted values were used to improve retrieval and disambiguation.

\subsection{Retrieval Grounding}

Recognized products were used as search queries against external product-information systems. Candidate products were ranked according to semantic similarity, textual consistency, and brand matching.

\subsection{Image Upgrade Verification}

Candidate replacement images were evaluated using similarity scoring and image-matching techniques. Only high-confidence catalogue images were accepted as replacements.

\subsection{Responsible AI Guardrails}

Guardrails assessed:

\begin{itemize}
\item Recognition confidence
\item Brand consistency
\item Restricted-item detection
\item Retrieval agreement
\item Image-match confidence
\end{itemize}

Low-confidence results were routed to human review rather than automatically published.

Snap-to-Sell improved identification accuracy, category classification, age-restricted-item detection, and image quality compared with the baseline. However, retrieval-grounded pricing performed worse due to ambiguity introduced by shopping-search retrieval.

\subsection{Failure Analysis}

The most common failure mode involved retrieval ambiguity. Products with visually similar packaging frequently matched multipack or alternate-size variants, leading to incorrect pricing.

Examples included:

\begin{itemize}
\item Single-item products matched to cases.
\item Promotional bundles retrieved instead of individual units.
\item Region-specific packaging variations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Discussion and Implications}

The results demonstrate that retrieval grounding improves factual consistency, category assignment, and safety capabilities. However, retrieval quality strongly influences downstream pricing performance.

The findings suggest that future systems should integrate barcode recognition, package-size verification, and stronger retrieval-ranking techniques.

\subsection{Project Summary and Key Findings}

Snap-to-Sell successfully demonstrated that a single smartphone image can serve as the basis for automated product-listing creation.

The system improved product identification, category classification, image quality, and restricted-item detection compared with a baseline workflow.

\subsection{Conclusions Drawn and Insights Gained}

The most important insight is that retrieval grounding improves factual consistency but introduces new challenges associated with retrieval ambiguity.

Successful automation therefore requires both strong retrieval mechanisms and human oversight.

\subsection{Recommendations and Future Work}

Future work should focus on:

\begin{itemize}
\item Larger retail-product datasets.
\item Barcode recognition.
\item Package-size verification.
\item Marketplace API integration.
\item Retail-specific model fine-tuning.
\item Multimodal retrieval improvements.
\end{itemize}

These enhancements may significantly improve pricing reliability and overall listing quality.
